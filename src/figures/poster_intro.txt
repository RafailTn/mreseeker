MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are small non-coding RNA molecules that function as guide RNAs, guiding Argonaute (AGO) proteins to mRNAs to perform endonucleolytic cleavage and trigger their subsequent degradation, contributing significantly to post-transcriptional gene regulation. We have utilized two existing datasets that originate from chimeric AGO2 eCLIP experiments to train and evaluate two types of Machine/Deep learning models, a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) and a CatBoost model. These experiments provide an immense number of miRNA-MRE pairs that have been directly isolated from tissues, something that alternative NGS and non-NGS approaches fail to achieve. The first dataset is an established benchmark on this task (miRBench, Gresova et al. (2025), HEK293T cell line), whereas the second comes from a SAEC cell line (GSE304955). The CNN model overlays the sequences within a predefined, biochemically inspired pairing matrix, prioritizing known base-pairing rules, while allowing for more complex patterns to be learned from the data. CatBoost uses hand-crafted features based on nucleotide sequence, biophysics and evolutionary conservation of the target. Given its tree-based structure, CatBoost draws feature specific thresholds for its decisions, thus being natively interpretable. Based on their Average Precision Score (APS) on miRBench, which is constructed to test model performance on unseen miRNA families, they outperform (CatBoost:0.83, CNN:0.87) existing State Of The Art approaches, like TargetScan, Agarwal et al. (2015), (0.76), in predicting miRNA-MRE interactions, while also generalizing to unseen cell lines. Due to their predictive capabilities and explainability, these models pave the way for better understanding of miRNA targeting, as well as for elucidating the effects of SNPs on this phenomenon.
