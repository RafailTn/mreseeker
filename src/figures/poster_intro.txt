MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are small non-coding RNA molecules that function as guide RNAs, guiding Argonaute (AGO) proteins to mRNAs to perform endonucleolytic cleavage and trigger their subsequent degradation, contributing significantly to post-transcriptional gene regulation. We have utilized two existing datasets that originate from chimeric AGO2 eCLIP experiments to train and evaluate two types of Machine/Deep learning models, a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) and a CatBoost model. These experiments provide an immense number of miRNA-MRE pairs that have been directly isolated from tissues, something that alternative NGS and non-NGS approaches fail to achieve. Due to their predictive capabilities and explainability, these models pave the way for better understanding of miRNA targeting, as well as for elucidating the effects of SNPs on this phenomenon.
